What Changed

Best Buy's Terms and Conditions, last updated Sept 25, 2026, now contain a "Digital Agents" section defining a Digital Agent as any AI-enabled technology able to take autonomous or semi-autonomous action on a person's behalf. The section requires Digital Agents to identify themselves as such in all requests, operate only through Best Buy's authorized methods, avoid mimicking human behavior or circumventing security measures like CAPTCHAs, and be used only by authenticated, logged-in customers. Best Buy reserves the right to limit, restrict, suspend, block, or ban any Digital Agent at its discretion. An independent archival tracker recorded 19 sentences added, 2 removed, and 38 modified around this date, but Redline could not confirm whether the Digital Agent section specifically was introduced in this revision or existed earlier — it is described here as current content, not attributed to this specific change.

Before — Prior version

Not yet independently retrieved by Redline. An archival tracker records a revision around Sept 25–26, 2026, but the exact prior wording is unconfirmed.

After — Current, dated Sept 25, 2026

Defines "Digital Agent," requires self-identification and authorized-method-only interaction, bars mimicking human behavior or bypassing security checks, restricts use to authenticated customers, and reserves Best Buy's right to block any Digital Agent at will.

Why It Matters

As AI shopping agents and browser assistants become more common, this is one of the more detailed Digital Agent clauses Redline has seen — it's worth watching whether other retailers adopt similar identify-yourself-and-behave-like-a-bot language, and whether "Best Buy's authorized methods" ends up meaning an official API versus a blanket ban on unsanctioned agents.

Who Is Affected

All Best Buy Properties users — purchases, financing, support, accounts, and guest use — and any AI agent or automated tool acting on a customer's behalf.
