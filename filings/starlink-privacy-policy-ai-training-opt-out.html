Starlink — Global Privacy Policy

Scope: Starlink Global Privacy Policy — all residential and roam subscribers
Updated: Jan 15, 2026
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Prior version: archived Nov 2025
Why this status: Reuters directly compared the archived and current policy text; Redline hasn't yet independently retrieved that archive to confirm firsthand.
What Changed

Starlink's Global Privacy Policy, updated January 15, 2026, added an opt-out (not opt-in) clause permitting use of customer data — including location, contact, financial, and "communication data" — to train Starlink's own or third-party AI/ML models. Reuters reviewed an archived November 2025 version and confirmed no AI-training language existed there.

Before — Archived Nov 2025

No AI-training language present, per Reuters' direct review of the archived version.

After — Updated Jan 15, 2026

Permits Starlink "to train our machine learning or artificial intelligence models" and to share personal data with third-party collaborators for the same purpose, unless the customer opts out.

Why It Matters

Because the clause is opt-out, subscribers are automatically included unless they act. For Starlink's rural and maritime customers, who often have no alternative ISP, opting out by canceling service isn't a realistic option — only the account-settings toggle is.

Who Is Affected

All Starlink residential and roam subscribers globally.

Notice Given
NO CLEAR NOTICE LOCATED

A support/FAQ article followed the change, but no prominent announcement was found at the time it took effect.

Community report

The change generated significant discussion on r/Starlink (a community of over 2 million members), where users flagged that the opt-out toggle isn't prominently surfaced, questioned the "de-identified where possible" language, and raised concern about data reaching Musk's other AI venture, xAI, given reported SpaceX–xAI merger talks.

— r/Starlink community discussion via Internet in Space coverage; not an official Starlink statement
