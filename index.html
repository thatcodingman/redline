REDLINE-2026-001 · Arbitration, AI Inputs & Fees

Google Ads — Terms of Service

Scope: Google Ads advertiser accounts (all regions); does not cover other Google product terms
Published by Google: Jun 1, 2026
Effective: Jul 1, 2026
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Why this status: Primary Google support documentation confirms the policy and effective date directly, corroborated by multiple independent trade-press reports.
First full overhaul since 2018 — Dispute Resolution, AI Inputs, Fees
Advertiser inputs into conversational features were not addressed by the prior termsmay now be used across Google Ads features to improve campaign performance, disputes move from the prior process toward standard venue-based resolutionto binding arbitration under AAA rules with a 30-day opt-out, and the liability cap shifts from a total-account-spend basisto a narrower per-account basis.
What changed

Google's first Google Ads Terms of Service rewrite in eight years pulls advertiser inputs (e.g. text typed into Ads Advisor) into its systems for improving ad performance, replaces the prior dispute process with arbitration, adds a clause permitting jurisdiction-specific regulatory fees as separate invoice line items, and narrows the liability cap to a per-account basis.

Community report

Ad-ops and marketing outlets tie the arbitration overhaul to a wave of mass-arbitration claims filed against Google Ads in May 2026, and advise advertisers to watch upcoming invoices closely for new regulatory-fee line items.

— Webtopia, ALM Corp coverage; not an official Google statement
Why it matters

A per-account liability cap limits recovery if a platform-wide error affects many advertisers at once — a narrower ceiling than the total-spend basis it replaces.

Adobe — General Terms of Use

Scope: Adobe General Terms of Use (Creative Cloud / Document Cloud individual & team plans)
Published by Adobe: Jun 5, 2024
Revised: Jun 18, 2024
Current version dated: Oct 3, 2025
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Related Filings — Adobe
  • REDLINE-2026-010 → — Oct 3, 2025 full General Terms replacement (this document later superseded the version discussed below)
Why this status: Adobe's current Terms page and multiple independent outlets directly quote and corroborate both the original and revised language.
Section — Content License / Improving Our Services
The initial June 2024 language let Adobe access content "through both automated and manual methods" to improve services; after public backlash, Adobe's revised terms left this broad framing in placeadded an explicit statement that customer content is not used to train generative AI, except content submitted to Adobe Stock.
What changed

Adobe's June 5, 2024 Terms of Use update added language permitting access to user content "through both automated and manual methods," which creators read as permission to train AI on their work. Following backlash, Adobe published clarifications and revised terms by June 18, 2024, adding an explicit no-AI-training statement, distinguishing unreviewed local content from scanned cloud content, and adding an opt-out for its product-improvement analytics program.

Community report

Coverage described a swift backlash from photographers, designers, and other creators, some of whom threatened to cancel Creative Cloud subscriptions over fears their work would train Firefly. Several outlets characterized Adobe's first clarification (June 6) as insufficient to fully quell concern, prompting the further revision on June 18.

— VentureBeat, Malwarebytes, No Film School coverage; not an official Adobe statement
Why it matters

The backlash shows what public pressure can accomplish, but it also shows how a single ambiguous phrase in a license clause — not a change in actual practice, by Adobe's own account — was enough to look like a policy reversal.

REDLINE-2026-005 · AI Training, Opt-Out Default

Starlink — Global Privacy Policy

Scope: Starlink Global Privacy Policy — all residential and roam subscribers
Updated: Jan 15, 2026
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Prior version: archived Nov 2025
Why this status: Reuters directly compared the archived and current policy text; Redline hasn't yet independently retrieved that archive to confirm firsthand.
Section — Third-Party Sharing / AI Model Training
The January 2026 policy adds language permitting Starlink "to train our machine learning or artificial intelligence models" and to share personal data with third-party collaborators for the same purpose, unless the customer opts out. Reuters directly compared this to an archived November 2025 version which contained no AI-training language.
What changed

Starlink's Global Privacy Policy, updated January 15, 2026, added an opt-out (not opt-in) clause permitting use of customer data — including location, contact, financial, and "communication data" — to train Starlink's own or third-party AI/ML models. Reuters reviewed an archived November 2025 version and confirmed no AI-training language existed there.

Community report

The change generated significant discussion on r/Starlink (a community of over 2 million members), where users flagged that the opt-out toggle isn't prominently surfaced, questioned the "de-identified where possible" language, and raised concern about data reaching Musk's other AI venture, xAI, given reported SpaceX–xAI merger talks.

— r/Starlink community discussion via Internet in Space coverage; not an official Starlink statement
Why it matters

Because the clause is opt-out, subscribers are automatically included unless they act. For Starlink's rural and maritime customers, who often have no alternative ISP, opting out by canceling service isn't a realistic option — only the account-settings toggle is.

REDLINE-2026-007 · Arbitration Opt-Out Removed

T-Mobile — Terms & Conditions

Scope: T-Mobile postpaid wireless Terms & Conditions; does not cover Money, Fiber, Business, or Prepaid terms
Prior version effective: Aug 1, 2025
Current version effective: Sept 1, 2026
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Why this status: Redline retrieved and directly compared both the Aug 1, 2025 PDF and the current live Sept 1, 2026 Terms & Conditions page, word for word. This started as a held candidate — see the Archive for the original hold record — and graduated to Verified once both documents were in hand.
Section — Dispute Resolution / Individualized Dispute Resolution and Arbitration
The prior Terms & Conditions let customers opt out of mandatory arbitration within 30 days of activating service or purchasing a device, by phone or via a dedicated websitecontain no opt-out mechanism of any kind in the current Dispute Resolution section. The related carve-out excusing opted-out customers from the Class and Mass Action Waiver is also gone, consistent with there being no opt-out left to carve out from.
What changed

T-Mobile's Terms & Conditions effective September 1, 2026 removed the right to opt out of mandatory arbitration that existed in the version effective August 1, 2025. The prior version let customers preserve their right to sue in court by opting out within 30 days of activating a new line or purchasing a device, using a named phone number or website. The current version keeps the same mandatory arbitration and class-action-waiver structure, but contains no opt-out clause anywhere in its Dispute Resolution section.

Unresolved discrepancy

T-Mobile's separate opt-out microsite, t-mobiledisputeresolution.com, still described a 30-day opt-out mechanism as of this filing's verification date — even though the controlling Terms & Conditions document no longer contains that right. Redline could not determine whether the microsite is simply outdated or whether some other mechanism preserves opt-out for certain customers, and is stating that plainly rather than picking an interpretation.

Community report

This change was originally flagged as a community-sourced candidate before Redline independently retrieved and compared both documents. No widespread news coverage discussing the removal specifically was found at the time of writing.

— Original candidate research; not an official T-Mobile statement
Why it matters

Removing the opt-out means customers bound by the current terms no longer have any contractual path to sue T-Mobile in court or join a class action over a dispute — arbitration becomes the only option. This is one of the more significant reductions in customer legal rights Redline has verified to date.

LinkedIn — User Agreement & Privacy Policy

Scope: LinkedIn members in EEA, Switzerland, UK, Canada, and Hong Kong (GenAI training); Microsoft affiliate ad-data sharing expansion is separately region-specific. No new AI-training change stated for U.S. members.
Document & Effective Date: Nov 3, 2025
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Why this status: LinkedIn's own Help Center page directly confirms the change, corroborated by seven independent outlets. Redline has not yet independently retrieved LinkedIn's September 2024 prior-version documents to diff the exact regional and affiliate-sharing language line by line.
Generative AI Model Training & Affiliate Ad-Data Sharing
For members in the EEA, Switzerland, UK, Canada, and Hong Kong, LinkedIn's data use previously followed the scope set by a September 2024 update, which introduced GenAI training language and an opt-out for a narrower set of regionsexpanded on November 3, 2025 to use profile details and public content from these additional regions to train content-generating AI models, and to share more member data with Microsoft for personalized advertising. Private messages are excluded from both AI training and ad-data sharing.
What changed

Effective November 3, 2025, LinkedIn expanded the regions where member profile data and public content can be used to train generative AI models to include the EEA, Switzerland, UK, Canada, and Hong Kong, and separately expanded the scope of member data shared with Microsoft for ad personalization in specified regions. Both changes are opt-out — enabled by default under "legitimate interest," with a toggle in Settings > Data Privacy to disable. Coverage was widespread and consistent across privacy-focused outlets (Help Net Security, MediaNama) and general tech press, all converging on the same effective date, regional scope, and opt-out mechanism — an unusually high degree of independent corroboration for a policy change.

— Help Net Security, MediaNama, and others; not official LinkedIn statements

This is a genuine expansion of AI-training scope for millions of additional members, but it was clearly announced by LinkedIn's own account — the more notable detail is that opting out doesn't retroactively remove data already used for training, meaning the practical window to opt out is before, not after, the effective date.

REDLINE-2026-009 · Cross-Platform Contact Discovery

PayPal / Venmo — Privacy Statement

Scope: U.S. Venmo personal accounts and PayPal personal accounts; Teen Accounts are handled under separate provisions
Document Date: announced ahead of launch, Sept 30, 2025
Effective Date: Nov 17, 2025 (Venmo Privacy Statement)
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Why this status: PayPal's own "Policy Updates" archive page directly quotes the exact language describing the change and its default-on nature, and Redline retrieved Venmo's current, dated Privacy Statement directly to confirm the effective date.
Cross-platform "Find Me" contact discovery
Venmo and PayPal accounts could not be found or paid across platforms using contact informationcan now be found and paid by users on the other platform via phone number (and later email), with matched accounts able to see name, username, profile photo, and country by default, unless the user changes their Privacy settings.
What changed

Starting November 2025, Venmo and PayPal users can find and pay each other using contact information, primarily phone number at launch. PayPal's own pre-launch notice states plainly that being discoverable this way requires no action — it's the default — and that users who don't want to be found this way must change their settings.

Community report

Tech press (TechCrunch, CNET, 9to5Mac) covered the feature primarily as a convenience update, but multiple outlets specifically flagged the default-searchable setting as a scam/spam risk worth disabling before the feature went live, since a phone number is enough to locate an account across platforms.

— TechCrunch, CNET, 9to5Mac; not official PayPal or Venmo statements
Why it matters

Default-on discoverability across a combined 2-billion-user network is a meaningful privacy exposure increase, particularly because it was rolled out as a convenience feature rather than framed as a privacy change — users have to already know to look for the "Find Me" setting to opt out before their number becomes a cross-platform lookup key.

Adobe — General Terms of Use

Scope: All Adobe websites, apps, Creative Cloud, Document Cloud, and software — the broadest possible scope, replacing and superseding all prior General Terms versions
Document & Effective Date: Oct 3, 2025
Verified by Redline: Sept 1, 2026
Related Filings — Adobe
  • REDLINE-2026-002 → — Jun 2024 AI-training backlash and clarification (the version this filing's document later superseded)
Why this status: Redline retrieved and read the complete current document directly from Adobe's own legal page. This is Partially Verified rather than Verified because Redline has not independently retrieved and line-by-line diffed the immediately preceding General Terms version — only the narrower June 2024 clause covered in REDLINE-2026-002.
Section 17 — No Modifications, Reverse Engineering, AI/ML (new section)
Adobe's General Terms of Use were entirely replacedsuperseded in full on October 3, 2025. The new document adds a dedicated section explicitly barring customers from using any content, data, or output received from Adobe's services — including Firefly generations — "to directly or indirectly create, train, test, or otherwise improve any machine learning algorithms or artificial intelligence system".
What changed

Adobe replaced its entire General Terms of Use on October 3, 2025 — not a targeted amendment, but a full document that "replaces and supersedes all prior versions" per Adobe's own heading. Notable provisions Redline verified directly: (1) a new Section 17 prohibiting customers from using Adobe outputs to train competing AI/ML systems; (2) Section 3.11(A) states that during a Beta Version, Adobe may analyze content — including human review — "regardless of whether you have opted out of data collection for non-Beta Versions," meaning the standing Content Analytics opt-out doesn't fully apply to beta features; (3) Section 16.1 states Adobe may modify or discontinue services in ways "detrimental or result in a diminishment of value to you... without liability to you or anyone else," with only "reasonable commercial efforts" to notify for paid offerings; (4) Section 14.6 sets a 30-day arbitration opt-out window from first use or from the date of the last Section 14 update, whichever is later.

What did NOT change

The no-generative-AI-training-on-customer-content commitment from the June 2024 clarification (see REDLINE-2026-002) is retained and restated in this version (Sections 2.2(F) and 4.3(C)(2)) — Adobe still excludes customer content from generative AI training except content submitted to Adobe Stock.

Community report

Redline did not find dedicated community discussion specifically about this full document replacement, likely because it was framed by Adobe as a plain-language clarity update rather than a substantive rights change — Adobe's own page states the update was made "to make the language easier to understand."

— Redline research; not an official Adobe statement
Why it matters

Framed as a readability update, this replacement bundles in a broad liability shield for Adobe's own service changes (Section 16.1) alongside a new restriction on what customers can do with Adobe's AI outputs (Section 17) — provisions with real legal weight, published under a "clarity" banner rather than as a rights-affecting change.

DoorDash — Consumer App Homepage

Scope: DoorDash consumer marketplace homepage (not the Dasher driver app)
Policy Type: App Update / Ad Placement (not a Terms/Privacy filing)
Announced: Jun 8, 2026
Effective: Jun 8, 2026 (separate rollout schedule not stated)
Why this status: DoorDash's own advertising and corporate pages directly confirm the new ad format and its stated purpose of replacing homepage banners.
Evidence level: Vendor announcement Archived comparison — not yet retrieved
Before/After comparison Pending — no pre-Jun-8, 2026 homepage build or screenshot has been retrieved yet. DoorDash's own advertising pages (linked below) show real creative examples of Spotlight in place — readers can view those directly, though Redline has not independently retrieved a pre-launch homepage screenshot to compare against.
What we know

DoorDash announced Spotlight on Jun 8, 2026, stated it replaces homepage banners, and reported (in its own investor press release) 2x higher click-through rates than banner ads in early testing.

What we don't know

The exact visual size difference versus the prior banner format, whether the April 2026 Reddit report of increased pre-listing ads is related, and how "early testing" for the 2x CTR figure was measured or over what period.

DoorDash Ads announcement — Ad surface change
DoorDash's homepage used standard banner adsnow features "Spotlight," a large-format homepage ad unit using static or video creative, explicitly replacing the prior homepage banners.
What changed

DoorDash introduced "Spotlight" on June 8, 2026 — a large homepage advertising placement that explicitly replaces the previous homepage banner ad format, per DoorDash's own advertising and corporate announcement pages. DoorDash's investor relations press release states Spotlight delivered 2x higher click-through rates than banners in early testing.

What did NOT change (as far as Redline has confirmed)

This report does not establish any change to DoorDash's pricing, delivery fees, or Terms of Service. A separate, unconfirmed community report about increased ads above restaurant listings is held below rather than merged into this record.

Community report

Separately, a Reddit user reported in April 2026 that more ads appeared above restaurant listings — but that report predates Spotlight's June 8 announcement and hasn't been officially confirmed as connected to it. Kept as its own held lead below rather than merged into this report. Independent trade press (Marketing Dive) corroborated the Spotlight launch and its stated performance claims without adding new figures of its own.

— Reddit user report; Marketing Dive; not an official DoorDash statement except where cited
Why it matters

A named, larger ad format replacing a smaller one — one DoorDash itself says outperforms the old format 2x on click-through — represents a deliberate, measured increase in advertising surface on the homepage, not an incidental one. That's a concrete, permanent record worth keeping even though DoorDash announced it clearly.
